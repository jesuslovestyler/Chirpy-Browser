Chirpy Engine 0.27 — the paint & motion finishing set test

Every effect on this page is drawn with CSS alone. Read down and check each numbered promise. Three honest notes before the promises: transforms are paint, never layout — a rotated or moved box leaves the words around it exactly where they were, and a link inside transformed paint is still found at its untransformed place. The ellipsis promise (#5) works by shortening the text itself, so selecting or copying that line gives you the shortened line, dots and all. And the two motion promises (#7 and #8) cannot be proven by a screenshot — they need your eyes, watching the page move.

1 — a soft box-shadow, and two shadows on one card

This card floats on one soft shadow — offset down, blurred wide, in a warm translucent brown. The shadow belongs under the card: it should never show through the card's own face.

This card carries two shadows at once: a hard green offset shadow sitting close, and a soft dark blur falling further away. CSS lists the first shadow on top, and so should the paint.

2 — text-shadow under the words

A heading with a low red shadow

This ordinary line wears a small pale shadow of its own, one point down and one across — subtle, but the letters should look lifted off the page.

3 — a hover background that arrives in layers

Rest your pointer on this card. At rest it is plain grey; on hover a warm diagonal gradient arrives over a deeper background color, and the words darken with it. Move away and the card is plain grey again — the layers lift exactly as they came.

4 — columns, with a headline that spans them all

The article begins in three balanced columns, the way the multicolumn set has always laid it out. Read down the first column, then the second, then the third, and the words run in their proper order.

Nothing about the opening stretch is new — it is here so the break in the middle has something honest to break. The headline below belongs to no single column.

A headline that spans the whole article

After the headline, the columns resume beneath it, taking up the story where the opening left off. This stretch and the next are a fresh run of columns, balanced between themselves, standing below the span.

That is the whole promise: columns, a full-width break, then columns again — one article, read straight through.

5 — the ellipsis line

This sentence is far too long for the narrow box that holds it, and it refuses to wrap.

The line above is set nowrap in a 250-point box with overflow hidden and text-overflow: ellipsis. It should end in "…" right at the box's edge — and remember the honest note from the top: the text itself was shortened to fit, so that is the line you would select or copy.

6 — transforms: a rotated badge, a grown box, a shifted box

Rotated seven degrees, words and all

The badge's red ground and its white words tilt together; the text around it does not move an inch to make room.

This box is scaled up from its left edge

This box is painted 34 points to the right

Both boxes keep their true places in the flow — only their paint travels.

7 — a hover transition (needs your eyes)

Rest your pointer here and watch: over about four-tenths of a second the card warms from blue to orange and glides 26 points to the right. Move away and it glides home again. No jump, no snap — a slide.

8 — keyframes that run on their own (needs your eyes)

This card breathes with a pulse — fading to four-tenths and back, forever, two and a half seconds a breath.

And this one slowly grows and settles, grows and settles — an alternate-direction keyframe run, scaling between its two stops.

If you have Reduce Motion turned on in macOS, both of these cards should simply rest, and the glide above should change at once instead of sliding — that stillness is the promise kept another way.

9 — inset shadows (new in 0.30)

This card's shadow falls INSIDE its own edges — a soft dark pressure along the inside of the top, as if the card were pressed into the page. Nothing of it shows outside the card's border.

This card carries both kinds at once: a soft outer shadow under it, like the cards of promise 1, and a hard green inset ring inside its edges — one list, each kind keeping its own order.

10 — steps() timing (new in 0.30; needs your eyes)

Rest your pointer here: the warmth arrives in four visible jumps, not a slide — steps(4) divides the change into four equal stands. Move away and it steps home the same way.

This card does not fade — it toggles. Its keyframes run on steps(2, end): two stands per run, full strength, then quarter strength, then back, about a second and a half a stand.
