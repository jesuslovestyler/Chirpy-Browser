Chirpy 0.29 — The Banding Repair

This page makes five promises about one thing: a background must paint as ONE surface, anchored once to its element's whole extent — no matter how the window happened to repaint it. Two of the promises you judge where you stand. Two ask you to scroll, because the fault they test only ever showed itself across repaints: a scroll exposes a strip at a time, and every strip used to re-anchor the picture to itself.

The little picture below is drawn for this page and travels inside it: a sunrise over two hill bands, the sun at 40% of the height, a cross standing on the nearer hill. Its landmarks sit at known heights on purpose — if the picture is ever sliced and restarted, the sun and the cross repeat, and you can see it at a glance.

2 — The same hero, after scrolling

Now scroll down until the hero has left the window, and come back up SLOWLY — a strip at a time, the way a trackpad drifts. Then look at the hero again.

What you should see: the same single continuous sunrise as promise 1. Before this repair, each re-exposed strip repainted the surface for its own slice of paragraphs and re-anchored the photograph to that slice, so the hero came back as a stack of restarted bands — several suns, several crosses. The repair makes every repaint compute the whole surface, so a strip can only ever lay down the same pixels the full pass laid down.

3 — A zebra list, honestly shaded

  • First item — no shading is declared for odd items.
  • Second item — shaded pale green, the whole item.
  • Third item — odd again: nothing behind these words, on any part of the line.
  • Fourth item — shaded, and long enough to wrap onto a second line, so its band must stand behind both lines and the space the item's own padding makes, one unbroken band from its top edge to its bottom edge.
  • Fifth item — odd, plain, unshaded.
  • Sixth item — shaded, plain and whole.
  • Seventh item — odd, and long enough to wrap as well: however many lines it takes, not one of them may carry a pale strip in its upper half, its lower half, or anywhere else. The shading belongs to its neighbours; none of it may leak.
  • Eighth item — shaded, to close the list.

4 — The same list, after scrolling

Scroll so the list crosses the window's edge a strip at a time — down a little, up a little, letting it repaint in slices — then read the list again.

What you should see: exactly promise 3 again. Before this repair, a repaint whose strip began halfway down an unshaded item could bridge its shaded neighbours' fill across only the strip's half of it, leaving a pale strip clipped to the lower half of the line and the upper half erased bare. That is the stray paint the 0.23 drill recorded and no one could prove; it should not be able to happen now, at any scroll position.

5 — A hover gradient, one flow

Rest the pointer on this box. Its hover dress is a single gradient, warm at the top and deep at the bottom, declared over several lines of text so the flow has room to show.

What you should see: ONE gradient flowing over the whole box, top to bottom. Before this repair, a block's state layers painted once per line, each line re-anchoring the gradient to itself — the box would have worn the gradient in bands, bright at every line's top. Now a block stamp's layers paint once, into the union of its lines.
